# Silicon Sample Benchmark — method registration form

**Entry:** Tier 3, **secondary-3**, literature-conditioned multi-model ensemble. The submission contains 208 average treatment-effect (ATE) forecasts covering 16 interventions and 13 outcomes.

Reproducibility materials for this entry are available at <https://github.com/lieblingszz/team28-tier3-secondary-3>. Full generation details for the GPT-4o, GPT-5.4, and Claude Sonnet 4.6 members are available at <https://github.com/lieblingszz/team28-tier3-secondary-1>.

---

## 0 · Approach identity and output

- **0.1 Team ★** — RAG team silicon sample study: Farah Adeeba (University of Konstanz), Jing Ma (University of Konstanz), Marcia Ferreira Goncalves (Graphwise), and Max Pellert (Barcelona Supercomputing Center). Corresponding contact: max.pellert@bsc.es.

- **0.2 Plain-language summary ★** — This entry combines literature-conditioned forecasts from GPT-4o, GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet 4.6, and Qwen3.8-27B. Qwen contributes two runs that differ only in retrieval ranking. These runs are averaged first, after which the four model-level forecasts receive equal weight. All members use the same global calibration factor with 0.35.

- **0.3 Submission tier and approach family ★** — Tier 3 direct forecasting with retrieval-augmented generation and model-level ensembling. No respondent profiles or synthetic survey responses are generated.

- **0.4 Pipeline** — Five complete member series are produced: two Qwen3.8-27B/HippoRAG variants and one series each from GPT-4o, GPT-5.4, and Claude Sonnet 4.6. The Qwen variants use identical inputs and prompts but differ between stock Personalized PageRank (PPR) and PIT-normalized `0.7 × graph + 0.3 × dense` retrieval. Each member is calibrated by `b = 0.35`. The Qwen pair is averaged, and the resulting four model-level series are averaged equally.

- **0.5 Coverage ★** — Complete coverage: 16 interventions × 13 outcomes = 208 ATE estimates.

## A · Scope of LLM use

- **A.1 Purpose** — The four model families directly forecast intervention effects using retrieved scientific literature. Qwen3.8-27B also supports knowledge-graph construction and fact reranking; Qwen3-Embedding-4B provides retrieval embeddings. Final aggregation is deterministic and uses no LLM.

- **A.2 Degree of automation ★** — Prediction, calibration, and aggregation are fully automated. No individual forecast cell was manually edited.

## B · Model and system details

### B-Qwen · Qwen3.8-27B

- **B.1 Model names** — Forecasting and retrieval LLM: `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B`. Embedding model: `Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-4B`.

- **B.2 Access and context mode** — Local inference through vLLM 0.19.1. Calls are stateless, single-turn, and contain one user message. Each request returns one completion. The final runs were produced on 30 August 2026.

- **B.3 Configuration** — Forecasting used temperature `0.5`, disabled thinking, and no specified seed, top-p, top-k, penalties, or stop sequence. Output limits were 600 tokens for comparative scoring, 110 for effect estimation, and 15 for fallback requests. For each survey item, three comparative scores were averaged; for each intervention–item pair, three ATE samples were averaged. HippoRAG operations used temperature `0.3`, a 512-token limit, and disabled thinking. The two retrieval settings were `graph_weight = 1.0` and `graph_weight = 0.7`, with PIT normalization and `passage_node_weight = 0.05`.

- **B.4 Customization** — No fine-tuning was used. Retrieval employed HippoRAG 2.0.0a3 over an OpenIE knowledge graph with PPR. The fused variant added matched dense retrieval while holding the model, corpus, graph, embeddings, and prompts constant. No external web search or hosted LLM was used in the Qwen retrieval path.

- **B.5 Persistent memory** — No conversational memory was used. Only the retrieval index, embeddings, and response cache persisted across calls.

- **B.6 Inference stack** — Python 3.11.6 on one NVIDIA H100 80 GB GPU with tensor parallelism 1. Qwen3.8-27B was served by vLLM 0.19.1; HippoRAG used Transformers 5.15.0 and Torch 2.5.1+cu124.

- **B.7 Ensemble role** — The two calibrated Qwen variants were averaged into one model-level series, giving each variant 12.5% of the final ensemble weight.

### B-Hosted models · GPT-4o, GPT-5.4, and Claude Sonnet 4.6

- **B.1 Model names** — OpenAI GPT-4o (`gpt-4o`), OpenAI GPT-5.4 (`gpt-5.4`), and Anthropic Claude Sonnet 4.6 (`claude-sonnet-4-6`).

- **B.2–B.6 Access, configuration, customization, memory, and inference** — This entry reuses frozen, calibrated forecasts generated by the Team 28 hosted-API RAG pipeline. All provider, prompt, retrieval, sampling, token, cost, and inference details are reported in <https://github.com/lieblingszz/team28-tier3-secondary-1>. No new hosted-model calls were made for this entry.

- **B.7 Ensemble role** — GPT-4o, GPT-5.4, and Claude Sonnet 4.6 each contribute one calibrated series and receive 25% of the final ensemble weight.

## C · Prompts

- **C.1 Exact prompts** — The exact Qwen prompts are included in the deposited reproducibility materials and are identical across the two retrieval variants. Prompts for the hosted models are documented in the supporting entry cited above.

- **C.2 System-wide instructions** — Qwen received the task definition, retrieved evidence, outcome wording and scale, and intervention text in a single user message. Comparative scoring considered all 16 interventions jointly. ATE estimation was then performed separately for each intervention–item pair, with the comparative score supplied only as supporting context.

- **C.3 Prompt-design rationale** — Holding prompts constant across the Qwen variants isolates the effect of retrieval ranking. The two-stage design separates comparative judgment from absolute effect-size estimation.

## D · Persona and profile construction

Not applicable. This Tier 3 entry uses direct forecasts and does not simulate respondents.

## E · Stimulus and survey administration

- **E.1 Stimulus presentation** — Models received the benchmark intervention texts directly; no respondent-level exposure was simulated.

- **E.2 Survey walk-through** — Forecasting was conducted for 44 raw survey items. Comparative intervention scores were obtained first, followed by intervention-specific ATE estimates. Item-level forecasts were aggregated into the 13 official outcomes, with `funding_perceptions` sign-reversed to match the official direction.

- **E.3 Response elicitation** — Comparative scores were requested on a −3 to +3 scale. Effect estimates were requested as numeric ATEs on each item's original scale. Unparseable estimates triggered a concise numeric-only fallback request. Token log-probabilities were not used.

## F · Stochasticity and aggregation

- **F.1 Runs and seeds** — Each Qwen run used three independent comparative-scoring attempts per item and three independent ATE samples per intervention–item pair. No fixed seed was supplied; exact regeneration is therefore not expected. The final aggregation is deterministic given the five frozen member series. Hosted-model sampling details are reported in the supporting entry.

- **F.2 Aggregation rule** — The two Qwen variants were averaged cell by cell: `Qwen_mean = (Qwen_stock + Qwen_g70) / 2`. The final estimate was `Final = (Qwen_mean + GPT-4o + GPT-5.4 + Claude) / 4`. Each model family therefore receives 25% weight. No performance or inverse-variance weighting was used because target outcomes and member-level standard errors were unavailable.

## G · Validation and post-processing

- **G.1 Human validation** — None. Individual forecasts were not reviewed or edited manually.

- **G.2 Post-processing** — All member series were required to contain the same complete 208-cell grid with no missing or duplicate values. Final ATEs are reported in original outcome units and rounded to four decimal places. The deposited submission passed all repository validation checks.

- **G.3 Calibration corrections** — All members were multiplied by `b = 0.35` before aggregation. Calibration was developed independently of the target study. An initial factor of `0.56` was reassessed using 40 prediction–outcome pairs from published Voelkel et al. data. A through-origin regression estimated an optimum near `0.321`; `0.35` was retained and reduced pooled RMSE relative to `0.56` (`1.123` versus `1.255`). The factor was applied uniformly to all models, interventions, and outcomes. No target-study outcomes informed calibration.

## H · Learning and conditioning components

- **H.1 Fine-tuning data** — None. No benchmark-specific fine-tuning was performed.

- **H.2 Context and retrieval corpora** — Qwen retrieval used 43 scientific papers divided into 1,132 passages. HippoRAG linked passages, entities, and extracted facts in an OpenIE graph and ranked evidence with PPR; Qwen3-Embedding-4B encoded passages, entities, facts, and queries. Both Qwen variants used the same corpus and graph. Corpus and retrieval details for the hosted models are reported in the supporting entry.

## J · Internal selection procedure

- **J.1 Design-space search †** — Retrieval comparisons considered stock HippoRAG and PIT-normalized graph–dense fusion while holding all other Qwen components constant. Aggregation comparisons considered a flat five-file mean and a nested model-balanced mean. The nested mean was selected to avoid assigning Qwen 40% weight solely because it had two correlated retrieval variants. No target-study outcomes informed these choices.
